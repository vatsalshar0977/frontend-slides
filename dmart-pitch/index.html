THE PROBLEM · 02
The shoppers who spend the most time and money are exactly the ones left standing.
Older, pregnant and diverse shoppers face long billing queues in the tight space around the counters — with no resting place. The study ties this to exhaustion and a poorer store experience.
The empathy map is blunt: at some point these shoppers "just want to bill and exit." Tired = shorter browse = fewer unplanned picks.
It is a marketing problem, not just a comfort one: a worn-out exit hurts repeat intent — the metric DMart lives on.

THE AHA
The study's journey map shows the resting problem surfaces only after ~1 hour — right at billing, when the shopper is nearly done. A fixed seating zone makes crowding worse. So we bring the rest to the shopper, on the trolley, at the queue.
Implication for DMart: the fix must live in the counter footprint — zero new floor space, zero extra queue.
